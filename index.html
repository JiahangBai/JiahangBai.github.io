I’m an undergraduate at Jilin University’s School of Artificial Intelligence. I spent nearly a year as an embodied AI algorithm intern at Dexmal, where I worked on model training and real-robot experiments.

One question keeps me curious: beyond scaling data and parameters, how can we expand the context robots can use to act, from current observations to past interactions?

As foundation models like GPT Astra become more capable, I see a key step toward physical AI in pairing strong vision-language models with action token representations that preserve what matters for control.

My first research project, S²Mem, explores how robots can retain fleeting visual cues and use them to guide later actions.

Research Interests

My central question is how vision-language-action (VLA) policies can use past experience to choose what to do next. In long-horizon manipulation, the current observation often leaves out facts that matter for action, such as an object’s earlier location or whether a step has already been completed.

Memory and sequence modeling for VLA

I study what to retain from a stream of visual observations and robot states, and how to retrieve it when it becomes relevant. I’m particularly interested in how a policy can retain brief events over long horizons without having to process the entire history at every step.

Representation learning for robot action

I’m interested in representations that preserve distinctions that change the next action, such as whether a grasp succeeded or whether a failed attempt calls for a retry. Real-robot tasks let me test whether those representations improve decisions when the current view is ambiguous.

S²Mem: From Sparse Cues to Decision-Relevant Memory in Vision-Language-Action Models

Jiahang Bai, Leyang Wang, Zixuan Huang, Wenxuan Guo, Xutao Sun, Kaixi Ying, Xiaowen Qin, Ze Chen, Erjin Zhou, Yixing Gao

Under review · First author · 2026

Robots sometimes need to act on something they saw earlier but can no longer see. S²Mem uses robot motion to select which visual cues to retain in multi-scale memory and trains the policy to recover masked past events.
